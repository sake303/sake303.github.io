Now

I'm a founder and builder. Most of my work starts with the same problem: body and performance data is easy to collect, but hard to turn into a good decision.

Founder. I'm building DLY, a decision layer for S&C and performance teams, so coaches and staff can turn body and performance data into clearer decisions about training, recovery, and return to play. I'm talking with the people who make those calls and testing whether that workflow can lead to a paid pilot.

Builder. I like to build in public. I co-host RalphQS, where people share experiments with AI and personal data, and I'm working on HealthMes Agent, an open-source, local-first health decision engine.

Before

Whenever I've moved somewhere new, sports have helped me find my place. I competed in taekwondo, studied kinesiology and sports medicine in Seoul, and later spent two years in digital health business development.

I've usually been happiest when I can make something and test it. As a kid, that was water rockets. Later it was a pose-estimation project, startup work, open source, and AI agents. That part hasn't really changed. I still build first and work out what I missed later.

Taste

I'd like to move between cities where sport is part of everyday life and stay long enough to know people there. I want to train, watch games, and host events as I go. I first got a real glimpse of that life in Singapore.

I keep most things simple and functional. I'd usually rather pay for access than own more stuff. I make exceptions for things that say something about me or hold a memory: sports and tech gear, or something tied to a place or community I've been part of.
